Filtering OTU tables

This section presents two important ways of cleaning the OTU tables:

  • First, by going through a global filtering to remove singletons, rare or scarce OTUs, which are most often artifacts (Sgarbi et al. 2020; Nikodemova et al. 2023; Brown et al. 2015). These also usually coincide with the OTUs with lowest BLAST accuracy (Tedersoo et al. 2022).
  • Then, by going through a taxonomy filtering, based on BLAST quality thresholds.

The two downloadable scripts below (004_global_filter.R and 005_taxonomy_filter.R) implement a standard approach for cleaning OTU tables based on abundance and prevalence, or based on taxonomy and BLAST quality, in a phyloseq object.

 

 

 

For a step-by-step explanation of each filtering procedure, see the sections below:

Global filtering

A global filtering - based on abundance of OTUs across all samples (singletons and rare features), or based on the prevalence of OTUs across samples (scarcity) - is typically applied early to remove very low-count and low-prevalence features that are disproportionately driven by sequencing/PCR error and index misassignment, thereby reducing noise and improving downstream compositional and multivariate analyses.

Singletons and rare OTUs

Singleton OTUs have been shown to be overwhelmingly composed of artefacts arising from PCR and sequencing errors, including chimeric molecules that are typically low-abundance and sample-restricted (Tedersoo et al. 2021; Sze and Schloss 2019). Their removal has repeatedly been shown to have negligible effects on hypothesis-driven community comparisons while substantially reducing noise (Edgar 2016; Nikodemova et al. 2023; Sgarbi et al. 2020). On the other hand, retaining them is justifiable in studies explicitly targeting rare biosphere diversity, but requires careful manual curation (Brown et al. 2015).

Rare OTUs (beyond singletons) can hardly be distinguished from artefacts, thus, low-abundance OTUs are often excluded from the analysis. However, filtering approaches vary among studies, and there is no agreed-upon consensus as to what filtering thresholds should be employed (Yu et al. 2017; Poos and Jackson 2012; Nikodemova et al. 2023). Nevertheless, rare OTUs, just like singletons, often represent residual artefacts, and removing them has minimal influence on community comparisons. Thus, filtering reduces the complexity of microbiome data while preserving their integrity in downstream analysis (Edgar 2016; Nikodemova et al. 2023; Sgarbi et al. 2020; Cao et al. 2021). Although rare taxa may in some contexts carry information on beta diversity, their retention is mainly justified in studies explicitly focused on rare biosphere ecology, where careful validation is required (Brown et al. 2015).

Rare OTUs are frequently removed using absolute abundance thresholds (e.g. <5 or <10 reads) (Edgar 2016; Brown et al. 2015), or based on relative abundance thresholds (e.g. <0.01%) (Nikodemova et al. 2023), while others filter rare OTUs based on statistical methods (Smirnova, Huzurbazar, and Jafari 2018).

Scarce OTUs

Scarce (low-prevalence) OTUs are features detected in only a small fraction of samples (<5%). The extreme sparsity of microbiome data sets poses a major analytical challenge and is largely attributable to sequencing artefacts, contamination, and/or sequencing errors (Cao et al. 2021). Thus, scarce OTUs are commonly filtered using simple rule-of-thumb criteria (e.g. presence in at least k samples or k% of samples), which reduces noise and improves statistical stability (Cao et al. 2021). This criterion does not relate to the relative abundance of the OTUs, and as such, rare OTUs may be prevalent microbial taxa.

In certain studies, higher thresholds for prevalence (>25% of samples) may be used to define, for instance, the core microbiome (Simonin et al. 2020).

Taxonomy filtering

Filtering based on taxonomy and BLAST quality is applied to remove OTUs with ambiguous or low-confidence taxonomic assignments, typically indicated by low sequence similarity, poor alignment coverage, or inconsistent rank resolution. Such features are frequently artefactual or biologically uninterpretable and can bias ecological inference if retained.
